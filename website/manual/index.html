How it works

SecretDoor is a password manager with no server. Your vault is a set of encrypted records stored in the app on your phone. You unlock it with a master password that only you know; the password is never stored and never sent anywhere.

  • There is no account and no sign-in. Install the app, create a vault and start saving.
  • Items are encrypted before they are saved. Names, usernames, notes and everything else are unreadable without your master password.
  • Because nothing is stored online, a lost phone means a lost vault unless you made a backup. SecretDoor cannot recover it for you.

That is also why SecretDoor is private: there is no company holding a copy of your data. The trade-off is that you are responsible for your master password and your backups.

Create your vault

The first time you open the app you create a vault by choosing a master password. It is the only thing protecting your data, so make it long. A few random words work better than a short, complicated password.

  • The meter under the field tells you how strong the password is. Aim for Strong.
  • Type it twice to avoid typos. Tap the eye icon to check what you typed.
  • The hint is optional. It is stored only on your phone and shown on the unlock screen when you tap “Show master password hint”. Never put the password itself in it.
  • Check known data breaches is off by default. If you turn it on, the app sends the first five characters of a hash of your password to a breach database, never the password itself. See Privacy.

Tap Create vault. If you already have a SecretDoor backup, choose Restore from backup instead and enter that vault’s master password.

Don’t forget it. There is no “forgot password” button. Nobody, including the developers, can reset or recover a master password. Write it down and keep it somewhere safe.

First-time setup

After creating the vault, a short setup offers three optional steps. You can skip any of them and come back later in Settings.

  • Set up unlock. Choose biometrics (fingerprint or face, when your phone supports it) or a PIN code so you don’t have to type the long master password every time. These are convenient but weaker than the master password: someone who knows your PIN and has your phone could unlock the vault.
  • Turn on autofill. Lets SecretDoor fill in logins in other apps and in your browser. See Autofill.
  • You’re all set. Tap Continue and your empty vault opens.

Your vault

The My vault tab is home. At the top the Types list shows each kind of item with a count, and tapping a type lists just those items. Below are your folders, the items that are not in a folder, and two special places: Archive and Trash.

  • Tap the + button to add an item.
  • Tap an item to open it. The … button beside it offers quick actions such as copying the username or password.
  • Deleted items go to the Trash first, so you can bring them back. Archive keeps items you don’t need right now out of the way.

Use the search icon at the top to find items by name, username or website as you type. Everything is searched on your phone; nothing is sent anywhere.

Folders (here Finance, Personal and Work) group related items, and the number beside each one tells you how many it holds.

Item types

Tap + and choose what you want to save:

  • Login: a username and password for a website or app, with optional authenticator codes and website addresses.
  • Card: credit or debit card details.
  • Bank account: account, routing, IBAN and similar numbers.
  • Identity: your name, address, contact details and Social Security number.
  • Driver’s license and Passport: their own item types, with fields for numbers, issuing country and dates.
  • Secure note: any private text, such as a Wi-Fi password or a safe combination.
  • SSH key: a private and public key pair. Learn more.
  • Folder: to organize your items.

Every item can also hold custom fields, file attachments and a note, and can be marked as a favorite with the star.

Add and edit a login

A login has a name, an optional folder, a username, a password and one or more websites. Next to the username and password are buttons to generate a new value and to show or hide it. When you are happy, tap Save.

  • Authenticator key: paste or scan the key from a website’s two-step setup and SecretDoor shows the live six-digit codes. Learn more.
  • Website (URI): tells autofill when to offer this login. The gear icon sets how strictly addresses must match. Learn more.
  • Check password for data breaches: an optional lookup that sends only a short partial hash, never the password.

Open a saved login to see it. Tap a copy icon to copy the username or password, the eye to reveal the password, the arrow beside the website to open it, and the pencil to edit. Copied values are cleared from the clipboard after the time you choose in Settings > Other.

To protect a sensitive item further, turn on master password re-prompt in its additional options: the app then asks for your master password again before showing or copying it. Learn more.

Cards, identities and notes

These work the same way as logins: fill in the fields you need, then save. The number and security code of a card are hidden until you tap the eye.

An identity holds personal details. Its Identification section has the Social Security number. Passport and driver’s license numbers live in their own item types, so they only appear on an identity that already contained them.

A secure note is a title and free text. Use it for anything you want encrypted that doesn’t fit another type.

Folders

Create folders from Settings > Vault > Folders or with + > Folder. To put an item in a folder, edit it, tap the Folder box, choose the folder and tap Save in the sheet, then Save again on the item. You can also create a new folder from that sheet with Add folder.

Deleting a folder does not delete the items in it; they move back to “No folder”.

The password generator

The Generator tab creates strong random values. Pick a type with the tabs at the top:

  • Password: set the length and which character types to include.
  • Passphrase: random words, easier to type and remember. Choose the number of words, a separator and whether to capitalize or add a number.
  • Username: a random username or a plus-addressed email.

Tap the circular arrows for a new value and the copy icon to copy it. Create login saves the generated value into a new login, and the menu at the top keeps a history of recent values. More about the generator.

Autofill

With autofill on, SecretDoor offers your saved login when you tap a username or password field in an app or a browser, so you don’t have to switch apps and copy and paste.

  • Autofill services: turn it on, and Android asks you to choose SecretDoor as your autofill service.
  • Copy TOTP automatically: when a login has an authenticator key, copy its current code to the clipboard after autofilling.
  • Passkey management and Privileged apps: for passkeys and trusted browsers.

Browsers need an extra step. See Autofill for setup and troubleshooting.

Locking and unlocking

Open Settings > Account security to choose how the vault locks and unlocks.

  • Unlock with PIN code (and biometrics, if your phone supports them).
  • Session timeout: how long the app waits before locking. New vaults start with 10 minutes. Pick a shorter time for more safety, or Never to keep the vault unlocked until you lock it. Session timeout action is what happens when it expires: Lock.
  • Lock now locks the vault immediately.
  • Change master password and Delete vault.

On the unlock screen, type your master password, or tap Show master password hint if you need a reminder. More in Security.

Be careful with Delete vault. It erases the vault from the phone for good, after you confirm with the master password. Backups you made earlier are not affected.

Backups

Open Settings > Vault > Backup. A backup is one .secretdoor file that holds your whole vault, encrypted as a whole. You need your master password to restore it.

  • Save backup file writes a backup wherever you choose: a Google Drive folder, a USB drive, another phone.
  • Automatic backup: pick a file once under Backup file and a Frequency (off, daily or weekly). SecretDoor then rewrites that file on schedule, even while the vault is locked. Back up now runs it immediately. A cloud folder that keeps old versions (Google Drive does) gives you several restore points.
  • Import backup adds the items from a backup file to this vault. On a new phone, use Restore from backup when you create your vault.

Keep at least one backup that is not on the phone, and try a restore once to make sure it works. Details in Backups.

Export and import

Export vault (Settings > Vault) writes your items to a file that other password managers can read. You choose the file format and confirm with your master password.

Exports are different from backups. A plain export is not encrypted: anyone who gets the file can read all your passwords. Delete it when you are done, and never send it over email or chat.

Import items restores a SecretDoor backup, or receives items from another password manager on the same phone through Android’s Credential Exchange. Importing CSV or JSON files from other managers is not supported yet. Learn more.

Several vaults

One phone can hold several vaults, for example a personal one and a work one. Each has its own master password and is encrypted separately. Open Settings > Vault > Manage vaults to rename or delete a vault, create a new one, or import a backup as a separate vault. When you have more than one, a vault picker appears on the My vault and unlock screens. Learn more.

Settings

  • Account security: unlocking, timeout, master password.
  • Autofill: autofill and passkeys.
  • Vault: manage vaults, folders, backup, export and import.
  • Appearance: language, theme and dynamic colors.
  • Other: clear the clipboard after a delay, and allow or block screen capture.
  • About: the version, this manual, the privacy policy, the source code, the license and open source notices, and the flight recorder for bug reports (learn more).

Under Appearance you can pick the Dark or Light theme, or follow your phone’s setting (the default). Here is the vault in the dark theme.
